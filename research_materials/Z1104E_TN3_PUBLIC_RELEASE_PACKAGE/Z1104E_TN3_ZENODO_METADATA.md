## Description
This technical note reports a fail-closed audit of whether multiscale organization of a two-dimensional microscopic model is sufficient to promote scale to an independent local spatial direction. The study separates information geometry, network geometry, derived operator locality, redundant multiscale coordinates, and physical spatiality through a sequence of preregistered positive and negative controls.

The tested constructions can generate nontrivial scale-dependent information geometry, higher-dimensional network connectivity, boundary-derived cross-scale operator response, and a boundary-induced presymplectic gauge-redundant representation. They do not, however, establish an independent local third spatial dimension.

Two bounded technical endpoints are emphasized. First, for a finite periodic massless nearest-neighbour scalar Laplacian on an N=2^m lattice with a standard periodized separable dyadic db1-db4 hierarchy, exact same/adjacent-resolution generalized intertwining forces the deepest physical stiffness action to vanish once its parent scaling space lies after at least two dyadic coarse-graining steps. The mechanism is a Fourier-alias invariant-core collapse. Second, for a fixed redundant db2 embedding, exact preservation of the boundary pullback and the boundary-invisible fibers uniquely fixes the induced bilinear form, and that unique form retains non-nearest-scale couplings.

The principal endpoints were independently reconstructed, and Proposition 1 underwent a separate referee-style mathematical audit that added explicit cascade-support and 1D-to-2D tensor-core lemmas. The note does not claim a no-go theorem for holography, MERA/cMERA, AdS/CFT, interacting quantum field theory, quantum gravity, or emergent dimensions in general. The novelty status of the bounded terminal-stiffness proposition remains unresolved.

## Additional note
The term “obstruction” is deliberately bounded to the frozen model class. The work should not be interpreted as evidence that the physical universe is fundamentally two-dimensional or that dimensional emergence is impossible. DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.22149735.
